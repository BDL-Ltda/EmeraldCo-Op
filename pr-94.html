Big picture

Replace cable operations, keep trade.c

Issue #80 asks whether the mailbox and server can carry the block-level operations used by the original game. This is a feasibility experiment. The ordinary bridge never sends the START command.

How it works

The original state machines keep running

The adapter exchanges real LinkPlayer identity and the original block buffers. It pumps from the main loop, so Summary, animation, evolution and saving can own the screen without stopping transport.

Under the hood

“Finished” has to mean something real

Select a condition to inspect why a screen can safely progress. These are the prototype's conditions, not production recovery guarantees.

Decisions

Three boundaries matter

Reuse the native screens

SendBlock → mailbox → trade_link
Why

The issue already chooses vanilla trade.c. The spike confirms its buffers and wait conditions can cross this transport without rebuilding the user interface.

Pump outside the overworld

CoopLinkSpike_Update()
Why

Menus and evolution replace the main callbacks. Transport tied only to the overworld would stop as soon as the trade begins.

Keep the experiment opt-in

START: role + fixture flag
Why

Normal play leaves the adapter inactive. Only explicit disposable-save fixtures replace the party and enable its menu; valuable saves must not use them.

How it was tested

Four paired emulator runs, then reload

leomayca used two independent mGBA 0.10.5 instances and a local server. Baseline and nominal 50, 100 and 200 ms added-RTT profiles completed the original trade, both evolutions, saving and field return. Restarting both games retained Machamp on A and Alakazam on B.

Both peers' recorded one-way settings were 25, 50 and 100 ms for the delayed profiles. This is artificial link-data delay plus scheduling, not a measured internet RTT. Raw desktop videos and saves remain private; the findings contain sanitized timelines.
